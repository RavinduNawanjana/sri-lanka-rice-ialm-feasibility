# Literature Context

This note provides external scientific context for interpreting the irrigation result. It is not used to impute missing GHG data or to replace local measurements.

## AWD yield and water use

Carrijo, Lundy, and Linquist (2017) synthesized 56 studies and 528 paired comparisons of alternate wetting and drying (AWD) with continuous flooding. Their meta-analysis found that the average yield response depended strongly on drying severity: mild AWD could reduce water use without a statistically significant yield penalty in many settings, while more severe drying produced materially larger yield losses.

Reference: Carrijo DR, Lundy ME, Linquist BA. 2017. *Rice yields and water use under alternate wetting and drying irrigation: A meta-analysis.* Field Crops Research 203:173-180. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fcr.2016.12.002

## AWD and non-CO2 GHGs

Zhao et al. (2024) reported in a meta-analysis that AWD reduced CH4 and combined CH4+N2O global warming potential on average, while increasing N2O. The magnitude varied with drying level, climate, soil properties, and management. A separate 2024 global meta-analysis likewise reported large average CH4 reductions, higher N2O, lower overall GWP, and a small average yield reduction, with the AWD threshold identified as an important moderator.

References:

- Zhao C, Qiu R, Zhang T, Luo Y, Agathokleous E. 2024. *Effects of Alternate Wetting and Drying Irrigation on Methane and Nitrous Oxide Emissions From Rice Fields: A Meta-Analysis.* Global Change Biology 30(12):e17581. https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.17581
- *Effects of alternate wetting and drying irrigation on yield, water-saving, and emission reduction in rice fields: A global meta-analysis.* Agricultural and Forest Meteorology 353 (2024) 110075. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2024.110075

## Implication for this Sri Lankan trial

The observed IR2 mean being 12.3% below IR1 cannot be interpreted without the realized water-depth trajectory. A label of “AWD” does not identify whether the plots experienced mild or severe drying, how many drying cycles occurred, or whether timing overlapped with sensitive crop stages.

Likewise, the external literature supports a **scientific expectation** that water management may materially alter CH4 and N2O, but it cannot tell us what happened in these 32 plots. The missing chamber data are therefore not an inconvenience that can be patched with literature averages; they are the empirical core of the mitigation question.
