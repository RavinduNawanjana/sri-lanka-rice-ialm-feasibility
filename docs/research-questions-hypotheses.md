# Research Questions, Hypotheses, and Estimands

## RQ1: Agronomic productivity

How does dry yield per plot vary across the coded tillage, irrigation, and nutrient-management treatments, and do any interactions appear large enough to warrant follow-on testing?

### H1: Tillage

The null estimand is no difference in mean dry yield between L1 and L2 under the trial's realized field conditions. The direction is not fixed a priori for confirmatory testing because reduced tillage effects are context dependent and the final assignment structure remains unresolved. The currently observed difference is reported descriptively, not used to redefine the hypothesis after the fact.

### H2: Irrigation

The null estimand is no difference in mean dry yield between IR1 and IR2. The direction is likewise left empirical because AWD yield performance depends on drying severity, soil conditions, timing, and management.

### H3: Nutrient management

Mean dry yield differs among F1-F4. Confirmatory contrasts should be prespecified after the final operational treatment definitions are reconciled. The current marginal ordering is not used to choose post hoc favorable contrasts.

### H4: Interactions

Tillage, irrigation, and nutrient management may interact. Because there are only two plot observations per coded cell, interaction estimates are secondary/exploratory unless additional independent replication exists.

## RQ2: Greenhouse-gas mitigation

Do the management practices change seasonal CH4, N2O, and relevant CO2/SOC outcomes relative to the appropriate baseline/comparator?

### H5: AWD and CH4

A literature-informed prospective hypothesis is that AWD will reduce seasonal CH4 relative to continuously/normally irrigated rice when implemented with effective drying cycles. This is a hypothesis only: the supplied source package lacks the gas series required to test it.

### H6: AWD and N2O

AWD may increase N2O under some conditions, so a CH4-only result is insufficient for a full climate assessment. Seasonal N2O and the combined climate metric must be estimated from measured or methodology-accepted data.

### H7: Tillage and nutrient management

Tillage intensity and nutrient management may alter GHG fluxes and crop productivity. Directions are not hard-coded because the effects depend on soil, residue management, fertilizer composition/timing, hydrology, and crop stage.

## RQ3: Feasibility under carbon-accounting rules

Can a practice that appears agronomically promising also satisfy active methodology requirements around applicability, baseline, additionality, monitoring, quantification, uncertainty, and leakage?

This is not tested by a single yield p-value. The methodology estimands are project-level compliance/evidence questions, not experimental treatment coefficients.

## Primary endpoints

Current agronomic primary endpoint: dry yield (kg/plot).

Prospective GHG primary endpoints after data recovery: seasonal CH4 and N2O per area, with climate aggregation defined only after the relevant methodology/version and GWP convention are documented.

## Secondary endpoints

Harvest-stage height, panicle density, seed/grain measures, water-use metrics if the AWD record is recovered, and yield-scaled GHG intensity.

## Multiplicity and model selection

A confirmatory follow-on analysis should identify primary contrasts before estimation, keep higher-order interactions secondary, and use the assignment-level error structure. Models are chosen from the design and measurement process, not from which specification produces significance.
